How it runs — the full sequence

The book operates on a 21-session clock: ranking decisions at each rebalance day's close, all fills at the next morning's open with 0.20% slippage and the full Dhan delivery cost model. Steps marked combo are what this preset adds beyond the aggressive config.

  1. Universe gates

    From ~2,000 NSE symbols, a candidate must pass all five on the decision day: 1-month avg turnover ≥ ₹8 Cr · close > SMA200 · close ≥ ₹20 · IFP ≥ 0.38 · ATR ≤ 5%. Typically 150–400 survive.

  2. Composite ranking — six factors combo

    Survivors are z-scored against each other on that day only: z(12-1m) + z(6m) + z(3m) + z(6m/ATR) + z(−base range) + z(52wk-high nearness), all weight 1.0. The sixth term is the combo's addition — George & Hwang (2004): stocks near their 52-week high continue outperforming; distance below the high is a headwind the composite now prices. Top 60 = keep-buffer, top 30 = target book.

  3. Sells — rank hysteresis

    A holding stays while it remains in the top-60 buffer; it is sold at next open when it drops out or closes below its SMA200. The 2× buffer prevents churn around rank 30.

  4. Capital, smile sizing & compounding

    Sizing capital = clamp(₹4L + realized P&L, ₹4L, ₹2 Cr), profit-only. Then breadth-smile: if 45–60% of the liquid universe is above its 200-SMA — the historical chop band with the worst forward returns — new-entry exposure is halved. At washout (<30%, historically the best forward-return state: +8.8%/quarter, 89% win rate) and in healthy trends (≥60%), the book runs full size. It never de-risks at bottoms.

  5. Earnings entry gate combo

    A buy is skipped if the company has a results broadcast within 14 days of the fill date; the next-ranked name takes the slot. A monthly-rebalance book cannot exit fast enough to dodge an earnings gap — so it simply doesn't enter in front of one. Solo effect: +1.7pt CAGR, −2pt DD.

  6. Position sizing & entry

    Buys fill free slots from the top of the ranking, weighted by 1/ATR% (risk parity — a 2%-ATR largecap gets ~3× the capital of a 6%-ATR midcap). Fills at T+1 open + 0.20% slippage + STT/stamp/exchange/DP charges. No hard stop-loss; exits come from rank, trend, and the volatility guard.

  7. Daily guard — relative-ATR trim

    Every session between rebalances: a holding whose ATR expands above 1.5× its own 20-session average while closing below its EMA21 gets 33% trimmed at next open. Volatility expansion during a rally is left alone — that distinction alone was worth +4pt CAGR over the static 5% ceiling.

  8. Cash yield combo

    Idle cash — smile trims, unfilled slots, gated entries — accrues 6%/yr, modeling a liquid-fund park instead of the 0% the backtest previously assumed. Pure realism; adds ~0.9pt CAGR with no trading change.

  9. Loop

    Proceeds and realized P&L return to the capital base; 21 sessions later the cycle repeats — ~185 cycles and ~2,300 round trips over 2011–2026.

The honest trade-off: the Combo does not dominate everywhere. Its gains come from transforming the weak regime — 2011–18 improves on both return and drawdown — while in the strong 2019–26 regime it earns ~2pt less than the pure aggressive config. You hold it because you cannot know which regime comes next, and its worst-regime Calmar (0.86) is the best of any tested configuration.

Live-trading notes

Plan with haircuts, not the headline. Apply the survivorship adjustment (≈ −2.4pt CAGR, date-aware delisting model) → realistic expectation ~20% CAGR. The Monte Carlo on this book family says a 35–40% drawdown at some point over a long horizon is the base case, not the exception — historical 24.5% is a favorable draw. Operational dependency: the earnings gate relies on the earnings_filings harvest staying current — coverage thins sharply after 2024 (2025: 3,958 filings vs ~14k/yr prior), so keep harvest_earnings.py scheduled or the gate silently stops filtering. The 6% cash yield assumes you actually park idle cash in a liquid fund.
